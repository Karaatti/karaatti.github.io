      Chemistry and structure

      Polypropylene is one of the most water-resistant common textile polymers. It repels moisture, dries quickly and resists many stains because the fibre does not readily absorb liquid.

      Its weakness is heat and lower melting resistance compared with some other furniture materials. Under friction and warmth, lower-grade fibres can flatten or fuzz.

      How it behaves in furniture

      It is especially useful in rugs and some outdoor-friendly textiles where spill resistance, low weight and cost efficiency matter more than luxury hand feel.

      For seating surfaces it tends to be valued more for practicality than richness. Designers often counter that by using textured yarns, mixes or pattern rather than relying on pure fibre character.

      Care and design watch-outs

      Moisture and wear note: Very low water uptake, which makes it useful for indoor-outdoor rugs and easy-care textiles.

      Care note: Keep away from high heat and vacuum gritty soil before it abrades the fibre surface.
