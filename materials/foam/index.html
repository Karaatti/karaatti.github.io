      Chemistry and structure

      Furniture foam is typically polyurethane built as an open-cell network. Density and firmness are separate variables: density relates to durability, while firmness describes how quickly the foam pushes back.

      Foam slowly ages through oxidation, repeated compression and heat. Better grades keep resilience longer, while lower-density foams soften and lose height sooner.

      How it behaves in furniture

      Foam is the core comfort material in seats, backs, headboards and toppers because it can be cut, layered and tuned very precisely for support, softness and edge definition.

      A furniture build usually matters more than the word foam alone. A high-density support core with softer wrap behaves very differently from a single cheap slab.

      Thermal and comfort behaviour

      Standard furniture foam usually sits between memory foam and latex in perceived warmth. It tends to breathe better than dense memory foam, but the exact result depends on density, perforation, cover fabric and the amount of body sink.

      Higher-density foam and poorly ventilated upholstery stacks dry more slowly and can feel warmer than lighter open builds.

      Care and design watch-outs

      Moisture and wear note: Open-cell foams can hold moisture; drying speed depends on density, airflow and cover construction.

      Care note: Keep ventilated, avoid prolonged damp storage and rotate reversible cushions when possible.

      Recycling and service life

      Recycling in Finland
      Use municipal bulky-furniture or mixed-furniture handling unless a local operator explicitly accepts separated foam.
      Expected wear profile
      Gradual softening and lower rebound over time; density is the main durability driver.
      Retail warranty note
      Material guidance does not extend the retailer warranty. Unless a product page explicitly states otherwise, keep the practical customer expectation at a 2-year retail warranty window.

      Finland-first sorting baseline: polyurethane furniture foam is not a plastic-packaging stream. Treat loose foam parts as municipal bulky furniture or mixed furniture waste unless your local operator publishes a separate foam route.

      When the foam is built into a sofa, mattress or headboard, prioritise reuse first and then direct the full product to municipal bulky-waste handling if separation is not practical.

      Foam durability tracks density more than marketing adjectives. Higher-density support foam usually keeps height and rebound longer than low-density commodity foam.

      Repeated compression, heat, oxidation and trapped humidity slowly reduce resilience. Well-designed layered builds age more gracefully than single low-cost foam slabs.
